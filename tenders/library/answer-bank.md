# Answer bank

Reusable answers to the questions that come up in almost every tender. Adapt
each one to the buyer's wording and the specific contract — a generic answer
reads as generic and scores as generic.

**How to use:** find the closest entry, copy it into the response file, then
rewrite the opening and closing sentences for this buyer and swap in evidence
relevant to their sector. Aim to change at least a third of it.

**How to grow it:** after every submission, move your best answers here with
the score they earned.

## 1. Mobilisation and implementation

**Asked as:** "Describe your mobilisation plan for the period between award
and contract start, including key milestones, resources and risks."

**What scores:** a dated plan, a named mobilisation lead, evidence you've done
it before at this scale, and honest risk mitigation. Vague reassurance scores
low.

**Skeleton:**
- One-line summary: mobilised and operational by [DATE], led by [ROLE].
- Milestone table: week-by-week from award to go-live, with owner per line.
- Resources committed during mobilisation, and where they come from.
- TUPE / staff transfer handling, if applicable.
- Systems, equipment and training in place before day one.
- Top three mobilisation risks and the specific mitigation for each.
- Evidence: "we mobilised [CONTRACT] of [SCALE] in [N] weeks, going live on
  the contracted date with [MEASURABLE OUTCOME]."

## 2. Quality management

**Asked as:** "How will you ensure consistent service quality, and how will
you measure it?"

**What scores:** a named system, the actual measurement cycle, what happens
when a measure goes red, and a real example of an improvement you made because
of data.

**Skeleton:** governance structure → what's measured and how often → who
reviews it → escalation when performance drops → continuous improvement with a
worked example → how the buyer sees all this (reports, meetings, portal
access).

## 3. Health and safety

**Asked as:** "Describe your health and safety arrangements for this contract."

**What scores:** competent person named, risk assessment method, training
records, accident statistics with a trend, and site-specific rather than
generic arrangements.

**Skeleton:** policy and who owns it → competent advice → risk assessment and
method statements for this contract's specific hazards → training matrix and
refresh cycle → monitoring and audit → incident reporting and investigation →
performance data (RIDDOR/incident rate over three years) → enforcement history
(be honest; buyers check).

## 4. Social value

**Asked as:** "What social, economic and environmental benefits will you
deliver through this contract?"

**What scores:** commitments that are additional (wouldn't happen anyway),
local to the buyer's area, measurable, and proportionate to contract value.
Anything you already do everywhere isn't additional and evaluators know it.

**Skeleton:**
- Two or three specific commitments, each with a number and a date:
  "[N] apprenticeships recruited from [POSTCODE AREA] within [N] months".
- Why these, based on the buyer's own priorities (read their social value
  policy — most publish one).
- How each is measured and reported, and who owns it.
- What happens if we miss a target.
- Evidence of delivering the same thing before, with the actual outcome.
