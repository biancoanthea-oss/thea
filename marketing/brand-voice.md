# Brand voice & claims policy

## Voice

Warm, plain, and practical. We are talking to two people who are three weeks
from their wedding and have a spreadsheet of unfinished tasks. Reduce their
anxiety; do not add to it.

**We sound like:** a friend who has done this before and is telling you the one
thing that works.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| "No app for guests to download" | "Frictionless guest onboarding" |
| "Set it up in 10 minutes" | "Enterprise-grade, scalable infrastructure" |
| "Every photo, in one place" | "Revolutionise your wedding media strategy" |
| Concrete nouns: QR code, gallery, slideshow, guestbook | Abstractions: solution, platform, experience |
| Second person — "your guests", "your wedding" | Third person — "couples can" |
| Contractions, short sentences | Semicolons, subordinate clauses |

**Tone by surface:**
- *Ad headlines:* direct benefit, no cleverness. The click is decided in a second.
- *Landing page:* reassuring and specific. Show the guest flow in three steps.
- *Blog/SEO:* genuinely helpful to someone planning a wedding, whether or not
  they buy. The Reddit-post test: would a stranger say "this actually helped"?

## Words we use

QR code · guests · gallery · slideshow · guestbook · upload · your photos ·
your wedding day · scan · no app · no login · download everything

## Words we avoid

Solution, leverage, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changing, unlock,
supercharge, revolutionise, "in today's fast-paced world", em-dash-heavy
copy, and any sentence beginning "Whether you're…".

Avoid "disposable camera" as a *product* description (we are not one) — it is
fine as a *comparison* ("the modern version of the disposable camera on every
table").

## Claims policy

**Rule: a claim goes in an ad only if it can be pointed at.**

| Claim type | Allowed? |
| --- | --- |
| Feature that exists in the codebase | ✅ Yes |
| Price stated in `brief.md` | ✅ Yes |
| "No app / no login required" | ✅ Yes — true, it's the whole design |
| "Your guests can't see or delete each other's uploads" | ✅ Yes — enforced by row-level security |
| Number of weddings, users, photos | ❌ Not until the number is in `brief.md` |
| "Best", "#1", "top-rated", "award-winning" | ❌ Never |
| Star ratings or review counts | ❌ Not until real reviews exist and are verifiable |
| "Unlimited" anything | ❌ Never — storage is bounded by the hosting tier |
| Competitor named in ad copy | ❌ Never in the ad text (Google policy risk and it advertises them). Comparison *pages* are fine |
| Urgency invented ("offer ends soon") | ❌ Only if a real deadline exists |

**Regulatory note:** UK/EU advertising rules (ASA/CAP, and the EU UCPD) treat
unsubstantiated superlatives and fake urgency as misleading advertising, not as
marketing flair. Every claim must be substantiable on request. Google Ads
policy separately disallows unverifiable superlatives without on-page proof.
